Leptospirosis: Pushing for Vaccination in Connecticut

November 17, 2026

A short guide to what the lepto vaccine's new "core" status means and where advocacy could save other dogs.

The short version

  • Lepto is now a core (recommended-for-every-dog) vaccine under the main U.S. veterinary guidelines.
  • No state requires it by law. Rabies is the only vaccine required in nearly every state, because it's a direct threat to people. It's almost always fatal once symptoms appear, and an infected dog can pass it to a person with a single bite. Vaccinating dogs is mainly a way to protect humans. The other core diseases are different. Distemper and parvo don't infect people at all. Lepto can, but it's treatable with antibiotics, and people rarely catch it from dogs. They usually get it from water or soil contaminated by rodent or wildlife urine.
  • I found no group campaigning to make it legally required. The most practical levers are boarding and daycare requirements, state disease reporting, and local awareness.

What "core" means

Vets sort dog vaccines into two groups:

  • Core: recommended for all dogs regardless of lifestyle, unless there's a medical reason not to. Examples are distemper, parvo and rabies.
  • Non-core: given based on lifestyle, location and risk.

Lepto was non-core for years. In 2024 the American Animal Hospital Association (AAHA) moved it to core. That followed similar statements from the American College of Veterinary Internal Medicine (ACVIM) and the World Small Animal Veterinary Association. The ACVIM recommends vaccinating every dog yearly, starting at 12 weeks.

The old belief that only rural or hunting dogs were at risk is outdated. A Chicago study found small dogs (under 14 lb) were more likely to be infected than larger ones. Unvaccinated dogs were over 20 times more likely to get sick.

"Core" is a professional recommendation, not a law. Lepto can also infect people, which is the strongest argument for treating it more seriously.

Where advocacy can make a difference

1. Boarding, daycare and grooming businesses

This is the most realistic way to make the vaccine "required." The ACVIM consensus statement recommends that facilities require lepto vaccination for boarding and daycare. Most haven't, partly because it used to be non-core, but some are starting to. Ask local kennels, daycares, groomers and training classes to add it, and bring the ACVIM/AAHA recommendation with you.

2. Connecticut disease reporting

Dog lepto doesn't appear on Connecticut's list of reportable animal diseases, so the state has no clear picture of how common it is. Under state law (CGS 22-26f(e)), the State Veterinarian (CT Department of Agriculture) decides what's reportable. Other places already require it: Michigan requires canine lepto to be reported within 7 days, and New York City within 24 hours.

A concrete ask: write to the State Veterinarian, and copy your state representative and senator, requesting that canine leptospirosis become reportable.

3. Local awareness

Personal stories make these messages land. Possible outlets:

  • The Connecticut Veterinary Medical Association
  • Local shelters and rescues
  • Your town's dog warden and dog license renewal mailings
  • Dog parks, neighborhood groups and social media
  • Local news. Connecticut vets have issued lepto alerts before.

AAHA publishes a free owner handout that's easy to print or share (linked below).
